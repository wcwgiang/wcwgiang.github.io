I'm a human factors scientist and UX researcher with 15+ years studying how people understand, learn, and safely use AI, automation, and other emerging technologies in regulated, high-stakes environments, spanning consumer finance, healthcare, and transportation. My work pairs rigorous mixed-methods research and advanced statistical analysis with usability evaluation and human-centered design.

I specialize in developing inclusive, accessible research approaches that support diverse user populations. I bring that research depth to product, operational, and safety-critical systems, translating evidence into practical guidance for technology design, decision support, and training.

Selected Research

  • Human–AI interaction & AI adoption How people learn to understand, trust, and adopt AI and automation, from in-vehicle automation to students' use of ChatGPT and large language models. Ran driving-simulator and eye-tracking studies of training strategies; partnered with industry, academic, and policy stakeholders to turn results into consumer-education and training guidance.
  • Health-insurance enrollment decision-making Mixed-methods research on consumer comprehension during employer-provided health-insurance enrollment, testing how decision-aid type, information structure, and framing shaped decisions, with actionable implications for improving decision aids in regulated consumer contexts.
  • Decision support & uncertainty visualization for medical dispatch Contextual research in a medical dispatch control center paired with controlled experiments and scalable online studies. Identified systematic judgment and misinterpretation patterns around uncertainty; outcomes informed technology transfer to a computer-aided dispatch vendor.
  • Inclusive design for medically vulnerable drivers Participatory design with older adults and individuals with mild cognitive impairment for highly automated vehicles, plus a large on-road clinical study of driver-assistance systems for people with Parkinson's disease, analyzing multi-modal datasets (video-coded behavior, telemetry, clinical assessments). Produced accessible interface concepts and inclusive-design recommendations.
